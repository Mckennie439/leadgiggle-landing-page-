Wondering If You Could Get More Out Of Your Ads?

A closer look at where ad performance actually breaks down, for businesses already running Google or Meta

Running ads doesn't automatically tell you why they perform the way they do. Most business owners running ads have a general sense of whether things feel "fine" or "not great," but not much more than that.

This document walks through where ad performance typically leaks, using the same diagnostic process behind LeadGiggle's own case study, and how to actually check, not guess.

1. Can You Actually See What's Happening?

Before anything else, one honest question: if asked right now how many leads came from ads last month, and what each one cost, could you actually answer, or would it be a rough guess?

Most of what follows in this document only works if that question has a real answer. Without it, it's impossible to tell whether the constraint is the ad, the landing page, or the follow-up, because there's no way to see where in that chain things are actually falling off.

Basic tracking, knowing which platform, which campaign, and ideally which ad produced a given lead, isn't a nice-to-have. It's the difference between fixing the right thing and guessing.

Self-check

If ten enquiries came in this month, could you say exactly which ones came from ads, and which specific campaign or platform each one came from?

2. Where's The Leak? (Click, Landing, Close)

If tracking is in place, the next question becomes where things are actually breaking down. It's almost never everything at once. It's usually one specific point in the chain.

Low Ad Click-Through
  • Ad media is unappealing (Meta)
  • Ad doesn't stand out in the feed (Meta) / search results (Google)
  • Ad isn't immediately relevant
  • Ad is too long or difficult to consume
  • Offer in the ad isn't interesting enough
  • Ad flow feels off, doesn't sound human (Meta)
  • Ad targeting is off
Low Conversion Rate
  • Page looks poor, weak attention to detail
  • Too much information, too fast
  • Media doesn't show real outcomes (Meta)
  • Not enough social proof
  • Not enough trust builders
  • CTA isn't clear or easy enough
  • Offer isn't interesting enough
  • Page doesn't load properly on mobile
Low Closing Percentage
  • Leads contacted too late
  • Whoever's contacting the lead needs sales guidance
  • Poor lead quality due to targeting or ad copy
Self-check

Looking at your own numbers, is it the click-through that's weak, the conversion once someone lands, or the close once a lead comes in?

3. Your Funnel Problem: Where Does The Conversation Actually Happen?

"Visit Profile" isn't automatically wrong. If the goal is awareness, staying visible, building recognition, it can be exactly the right choice.

The mismatch happens when the goal is actually leads or bookings, but the CTA still sends someone into a general profile instead of continuing the specific thing that got them to click. The ad made a promise. The click should land somewhere that keeps that promise going, not somewhere the visitor has to start over figuring out why they clicked.

This matters just as much for DMs. A DM conversation might work fine for a lower-ticket, quick-decision purchase. For a high-ticket, considered decision, negotiating a large project entirely through chat can quietly work against the sale, there's no clear structure, no natural point where the conversation moves toward a real next step.

Self-check

What's this campaign actually trying to do, build awareness, or generate direct enquiries? And does the CTA match that goal, or is it defaulting to whichever option was easiest to set up?

4. Lead Quality Is Poor

Getting enquiries isn't the same as getting the right ones. This is often where the real leak hides, everything upstream might be working, and the business still ends up with a pipeline full of tire-kickers.

Common Causes, Google

  • Ads sending traffic to a general homepage instead of a dedicated landing page
  • A form that only asks for name, phone, email, and service type, capturing contact details but filtering nothing

Common Causes, Meta

  • Running "Send Message" ads instead of "Learn More" ads pointed at a dedicated landing page
  • No qualifying step before a conversation starts, every message treated the same regardless of intent

What's actually happening underneath these

For Google, the fix usually starts with where the ad sends people. A dedicated landing page, built around one specific offer and real trust signals, gives a visitor a reason to act and filters out people who were never a serious fit. The form itself matters just as much. Adding two or three sharper questions, project scope, timeline, budget range, does real qualifying work before a conversation even starts.

For Meta, the pattern worth understanding is why "Send Message" ads tend to produce this specific problem. The Meta Pixel optimizes for whatever signal it's given. If the only signal is "someone sent a message," that's all it learns to chase, more people who message, with no way to tell a serious buyer from someone who messaged out of curiosity. Over time, this can quietly train the algorithm to find more of the wrong kind of lead, while the sales team spends real time following up on enquiries that were never going anywhere. A tighter landing page with a proper form usually means fewer total enquiries, but meaningfully better ones, and better signal for the Pixel to actually learn from.

Worth noting: WhatsApp-based ads can be made to perform better with the right setup, feeding qualified-lead data back into Meta so the Pixel learns from actual outcomes, not just messages sent. Most businesses running WhatsApp ads haven't set this up, which is usually why the pattern above shows up in the first place.

Self-check

If ten enquiries came in this week, how many would you honestly call serious buyers? And is the current setup, ad type, landing destination, form, actually built to filter for that, or just built to make it easy to enquire?

5. Campaign Structure & Budget Allocation

Even a strong ad and a strong landing page can underperform if the campaign itself is structured poorly underneath them.

Is spend split across so many campaigns or audiences that none of them ever get enough budget to actually exit the learning phase and produce reliable data? This is the same problem as spreading a small budget across too many platforms, just happening inside a single one.

There's also a subtler version of this: multiple campaigns unintentionally targeting the same audience, competing against each other rather than reaching new people, which quietly drives up costs without adding real reach.

Self-check

How many active campaigns or ad sets are currently running, does each one have enough budget to actually prove whether it works, and are any of them likely competing for the exact same audience?

6. Creative Testing Discipline

Running the same ad for months without ever testing a variation is a common, quiet cause of decline. Audiences get used to seeing the same thing, and performance drops even when nothing else about the business has changed.

Signs Of Creative Fatigue Inside The Account

Frequency is the clearest signal, how many times, on average, the same person has seen the ad. It behaves differently depending on the campaign type, so the same number doesn't mean the same thing everywhere.

For cold audiences, people who haven't engaged with the business before, performance typically starts declining once frequency passes 2.5 to 3.0. For retargeting audiences, people who've already shown interest, a higher frequency is normal and expected, generally safe up to around 4 to 6, since familiarity works differently once someone already knows the business.

Alongside frequency, two other patterns are worth checking: click-through rate declining over several days without any other change made, and cost per result climbing while spend and targeting stay the same. Any one of these alone isn't proof, but two or more moving together, especially with frequency climbing at the same time, usually means the creative has run its course.

If Creative Is Underperforming, Where To Look For Something Fresh

Best-performing organic social posts make strong test creative. If something already earned real engagement once, comments, shares, saves, that's a signal worth testing as a paid ad, not just leaving in the feed.

Video testimonials tend to perform especially well for home service businesses. They're harder to fake than a written review and easier for a stranger to trust, and most competitors still aren't using them.

For home service businesses specifically, before-and-after content tends to be some of the strongest creative available. It's concrete, it's proof, and it does the persuading almost on its own, in a category where most competitors are still leading with generic stock imagery or plain text.

Self-check

Looking at the account, is click-through declining, cost climbing, or frequency creeping up over the past few weeks? And is there a best-performing organic post, video testimonial, or before-and-after project that's never actually been tested as a paid ad?

7. Platform-Specific Nuance: Google vs. Meta

Google and Meta don't fail for the same reasons.

On Google, the person searching already has intent. If click-through is weak, it's usually relevance, wrong keywords, or the ad copy not matching what was searched. If conversion is weak, the landing page usually isn't answering the exact question that search implied.

On Meta, nobody was looking for this a moment ago. If click-through is weak, it's almost always the creative itself, not compelling enough to interrupt a scroll. If conversion is weak, it's often a trust gap, the page doesn't yet give someone unfamiliar with the business enough reason to believe it.

Self-check

Which platform is this, and does the likely cause match what's typical for that platform, or does something about the pattern feel different from what's expected?

Where Is Your Ad Constraint?

If tracking isn't in place, that's the actual starting point, everything else in this document depends on being able to see the numbers first.

If click-through is weak, the ad itself isn't earning attention.

If conversion is weak, the page isn't giving someone a reason to act once they land.

If lead quality is poor, the funnel is filtering for volume, not fit.

If closing is weak, the leak isn't in the ad at all, it's in what happens after someone reaches out.

If the funnel itself, DM-only, "Visit Profile" on a leads campaign, is working against the goal, that's worth fixing before anything else gets tested further.

If none of the above quite fits, the issue might be sitting underneath all of it, campaign structure spreading budget too thin, campaigns competing against each other, or creative that's been running unchanged for too long.

Seeing where the leak is is one thing. Knowing exactly what to do about it is a different exercise entirely.
